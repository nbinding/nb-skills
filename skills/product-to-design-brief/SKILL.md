# Product to Design Brief

Transform product documentation into structured, comprehensive UX/UI design briefs.

## Process

**1. Ask first who the brief is for.** Before writing anything, ask the user whether the brief is intended for an **LLM tool** (Figma Make, Claude Design, Google Stitch, etc.) or for a **human designer** — this changes both the level of detail and the tone, so it's not a question worth guessing at.

**2. Accept** product ideas, PDDs, PRDs, or any product documentation as input.

**3. Transform** the input into a comprehensive design brief following the UX/UI Design Brief Template structure below.

**4. Use British English** spelling and grammar throughout (e.g. "colour" not "color", "optimise" not "optimize", "centre" not "center").

**5. Populate all 12 sections** of the template:

1. Brief Metadata
2. Product Context
3. Scope and Design Constraints
4. Design System and Visual Direction
5. Screens or Surfaces
6. States and Edge Cases
7. Interactions and Motion
8. Accessibility and Responsive Requirements
9. Tone and Copy Guidance
10. User Flows
11. References and Inputs
12. Copy-Paste Prompt for LLM or AI Design Tool

**6. Remove any sections that are not applicable** rather than leaving them empty.

**7. Be specific and concrete** — avoid vague instructions like "make it clean" or "use best judgement".

**8. Separate constraints from aspirations**, and describe outcomes rather than just components.

**9. For LLM-targeted briefs**, ensure the final section (12) contains a complete, copy-paste-ready prompt.

**10. For human designer briefs**, focus on clarity, completeness and actionable guidance.

## Standard

Always maintain a professional, precise tone. Ensure the brief is specific enough that a designer or an LLM doesn't have to guess about layout, state handling, access rules, or visual direction. Use exact values where they matter.

## Rules

- Maintain clarity and formatting in your responses.
- Use British English spelling and grammar at all times.
